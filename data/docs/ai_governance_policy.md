# AI Governance Policy

## High-Risk AI Definition

An AI system is high risk when it meets the definition `automated decision + material impact on individual`. Examples include automated credit decisions, adverse eligibility recommendations, fraud restrictions, or customer treatment decisions that materially affect access, pricing, or service.

## Human Oversight

High-risk AI must include meaningful human oversight, documented escalation paths, and authority for reviewers to override automated outputs. Human reviewers must have access to evidence, model limitations, and decision rationale.

## Fairness Testing

AI systems used in customer-impacting workflows require fairness testing before deployment and during monitoring. Testing must evaluate protected-class proxies where legally permissible, adverse impact indicators, and segment-level performance differences.

## Explainability Requirements

AI outputs must provide an explanation appropriate to the use case, model type, and stakeholder. For high-risk AI, explanations must support customer communication, internal review, and independent challenge by model risk or compliance teams.

## Responsible AI Controls

Controls include data lineage, prompt governance, retrieval evidence retention, human review, incident reporting, model monitoring, and periodic control testing. High-risk AI controls must be reviewed with model risk management before production deployment.

## Governance Escalation

Unapproved use of high-risk AI must be escalated to AI governance, legal, compliance, and model risk. Deployment must pause when unsupported claims, missing oversight, or material fairness concerns are identified.
