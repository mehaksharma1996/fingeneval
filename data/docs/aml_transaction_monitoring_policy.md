# AML Transaction Monitoring Policy

## Suspicious Transaction Indicators

Suspicious indicators include structuring, rapid movement of funds, activity inconsistent with customer profile, use of high-risk jurisdictions, unexplained third-party transfers, and transactions designed to avoid reporting thresholds. Alerts must be supported by transaction detail and customer due diligence information.

## Alert Escalation

Level 1 analysts review alerts for false positives, customer profile consistency, and available documentation. Alerts with credible suspicion must be escalated to Level 2 investigations within two business days. Level 2 investigators determine whether enhanced due diligence or SAR consideration is required.

## Enhanced Due Diligence

Enhanced due diligence is required for politically exposed persons, high-risk jurisdictions, complex ownership structures, and repeated unusual activity. Investigators must document source of funds, expected activity, beneficial ownership, and any mitigating explanation.

## SAR Filing Timelines

When suspicion is established, AML SAR filing must occur `within 30 days of suspicion`, `60 days maximum`. The 60-day maximum applies only when additional information is needed to identify a suspect or complete required documentation. Missed SAR timelines must be escalated to AML compliance leadership.

## Governance Requirements

AML monitoring scenarios must be tuned, tested, and reviewed by compliance analytics. Material scenario changes require approval from AML governance and evidence of alert quality monitoring.
